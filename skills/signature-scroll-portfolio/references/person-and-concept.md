# From a person to a concept

Use this before storyboarding and when correcting portfolio copy. The intake
prompt in the README helps collect material; it does not verify it.

## 1. Build two separate records

### Private fact ledger

Record the claim, source, date, confidence, current status and publication
permission. Read long exports in bounded, non-overlapping sections and keep a
coverage index. Preserve disagreement instead of silently choosing the most
impressive claim.

| Fact | Evidence | Status | Public treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Built an attendance prototype | Owner statement + accessible code | Prototype, not proven paid deployment | Describe the actual contribution |
| “Thousands of users” | Unattributed old AI summary | Unverified | Omit until confirmed |
| Confidential client work | Private source | Owner-confirmed contribution | Generic experience only if authorized |
| Qualification | Résumé/owner dates | In progress | Say “pursuing”, not “graduate” |

Do not upload the ledger to image/video services. They usually need a sanitized
visual brief and specifically approved references, not a biography.

### Public content map

Use only approved facts. Separate:

- Opening: name, role, concrete positioning and next action.
- Selected work: a small strong set with understandable problems and contributions.
- Other work: compact, optionally expandable entries rather than giant repetitive cards.
- Experience: distinguish employment, internship, research, personal practice and learning.
- Skills: show where a skill was used; do not present interests as mastery.
- Education: one clear source of truth, not repeated in two sections.
- About: a brief human explanation of how the owner approaches work.
- Contact/social links and a factual downloadable résumé if requested.

Public repository visibility proves accessibility of that repository, not the
truth of every claim in it. Public permission for a project does not authorize
disclosure of a private successor or unrelated client.

## 2. Define the audience before the spectacle

Ask: who needs to understand the owner, what decision are they making, and how
much attention will they reasonably give? A recruiter should reach the work
directly. A prospective client should see scope, process and outcomes. A creative
peer may choose the cinematic route, but that route is not compulsory.

Write a remembered sentence, for example:

> “This person turns field observations into maintainable mapping tools.”

Avoid a vague sentence such as “a passionate innovator transforming tomorrow.”
Every section should help substantiate the remembered sentence.

## 3. Find a signature without inventing a persona

Look for owner-confirmed signals: repeated problem-solving interests, a specific
craft, an artifact they made, a meaningful tool, a recurring work process, their
stated creative taste. A motif need not be rare; its connection to the person
must be specific.

Offer two or three genuinely different directions when no direction exists.
For each, include the connection to the owner, opening image, narrative change,
how actual work appears, mobile plan, static plan, risks and estimated production
effort. Do not merely recolour the same concept.

Examples, **not templates to impose**:

- Ceramic artist: a real clay gesture resolves into the shapes of selected work;
  macro texture, soft daylight, no chip or computer-generated face needed.
- Accessibility engineer: a tangled interface becomes an understandable structure;
  readable before/after interaction, restrained motion, the demonstration itself
  carries the story.
- Field researcher: a notebook observation develops into a map and an evidence
  trail; original field material only with consent and location/privacy review.
- Music producer: a recorded gesture becomes a visual arrangement; sound remains
  optional and the story still works muted.
- Hemanth: his supplied portrait/eye becomes a fictional journey into thinking
  and building. Brown eyes alone were not evidence of a personality or ability.

Reject an idea if the metaphor could be applied unchanged to almost anyone, if
it cannot reveal the work, or if it relies on an expensive fragile effect that
adds little meaning. A quiet editorial site can be the better signature.

## 4. Turn taste into constraints

Collect a few references and ask **what** the owner likes in them: composition,
scale, type, pacing, material, atmosphere, interaction or tone. Separate those
traits from copying another site's identity, assets or exact layout.

Record:

- Palette roles, contrast targets and unwanted colours.
- Typography mood and readable sizes, not arbitrary prestige font choices.
- Dense versus spacious layout, information priorities and mobile hierarchy.
- Motion intensity, avoided effects, sound preference and skip paths.
- Photographic identity invariants and permitted edits.
- Maximum complexity, production budget and expected network/device conditions.

The concept brief should fit on a page. Keep the original fact ledger private;
the brief sent to external creative services should contain only the minimum
authorized information.

## 5. Write copy someone would actually say

Use the owner's public speech and writing as a guide to directness, sentence
length and vocabulary. Do not reproduce typing mistakes to manufacture
authenticity. Avoid repeated grand claims, generic motivational lines and
model-facing annotations such as “résumé-stated” in the final interface.

Removing those annotations does not authorize inflating the claim. Either
verify the underlying statement, write a narrower true statement, or leave it
out. Keep evidence notes in the private ledger.

Prefer:

> “I built the ingestion pipeline and worked on retry handling.”

over:

> “Revolutionized data workflows through a cutting-edge intelligent ecosystem.”

Don't claim all work was unaided or entirely hand-written when AI assistance
was involved. First-person authorship and genuine creative direction can coexist
with an accurate account of the tools used.

## 6. Resume and professional proof

If requested, derive a concise résumé from the same verified public content.
Use a readable single-column structure for a one-page early-career résumé when
appropriate; one page is a user/audience choice, not a universal law.

Check text extraction, links, selectable text, print size, clipping and the
actual PDF—not only its source. Keep title, dates and project status consistent
with the website. Make preview and download easy. Do not add fabricated ATS
scores, unverifiable metrics, proficiency percentages or decorative clutter that
hides the relevant work.

## Output before generation

Prepare `creative-direction.md`, a public content map and a short unresolved
decision list. Preserve the owner's approvals. A new assistant should be able
to tell what is fixed, what is experimental and what is not allowed to be public.
